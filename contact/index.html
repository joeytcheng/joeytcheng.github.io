FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Questions about joining the lab

Are you accepting graduate students?

I review applications from students whose interests fit closely with the Human Connection Lab’s research areas, including leadership, status, gender disparities in groups, loneliness, social isolation, culture, and computational approaches to social behaviour.

Availability varies from year to year. Prospective graduate students should consult the relevant York University program information and briefly explain how their interests connect with the lab’s current research.

What kinds of research projects do students work on?

Student projects may examine leadership and status in groups, gender disparities, social connection and loneliness, cultural variation, or the ways social ideas are expressed in public discourse.

Depending on the project, students may work with experiments, surveys, group-interaction data, multinational datasets, archival material, natural-language data, or computational methods.

Can undergraduate students join the lab?

Undergraduate opportunities may be available through independent research projects, honours theses, volunteer research positions, or paid research-assistant roles.

Opportunities depend on current projects, supervision capacity, relevant experience, and the amount of time a student can commit. Interested students should complete the lab application rather than sending only a general expression of interest.

What should I include when contacting you about research opportunities?

Briefly describe your academic background, research interests, relevant coursework or experience, and the specific parts of the lab’s work that interest you.

It is also helpful to include your expected availability, the type of opportunity you are seeking, and a current résumé or CV when appropriate.

Do I need prior experience with statistics or programming?

Prior experience is useful but is not required for every role. Different projects require different skills, and some positions are suitable for students who are still developing their research methods, statistics, or programming background.

Applicants should accurately describe their current experience with tools such as R, Python, SPSS, Qualtrics, data management, or qualitative coding. Interest in learning new methods is also important.

Where is the lab located?

The Human Connection Lab is based in the Department of Psychology at York University in Toronto, Ontario.

The format of lab activities varies by project. Some work may take place in person, while other meetings and research tasks may be completed remotely or in a hybrid format.

Who should contact you for media or speaking inquiries?

Journalists, producers, event organizers, researchers, and community organizations are welcome to contact me directly by email.

Please include the topic, intended audience, format, expected time commitment, deadline, and any relevant background information.
