  <section class="faq-section" id="faq">

    <div class="faq-heading">
      <p class="contact-eyebrow">FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS</p>
      <h2>Questions about joining the lab</h2>
    </div>

    <div class="faq-list">

      <details class="faq-item">
        <summary>Are you accepting graduate students?</summary>

        <div class="faq-answer">
          <p>
            Yes, I am actively recruiting prospective MA and PhD students
            through York University’s Social and Personality Psychology
            program.
          </p>

          <p>
            I am especially interested in applicants whose interests fit
            closely with the Human Connection Lab’s research on social
            connection and loneliness, leadership and status, gender
            disparities in groups, cultural norms, and computational
            approaches to understanding social behaviour.
          </p>

          <p>
            The deadline to submit a complete application to the York
            University Graduate Program in Psychology is December 1.
            Applications typically open in mid-October. Because applicants
            must arrange references and prepare several supporting documents,
            I strongly recommend beginning the process early.
          </p>

          <p>
            Prospective applicants are encouraged to email me in late summer
            or early September, before applying. Please briefly describe your
            academic background, research interests, relevant research or
            technical experience, and how your interests connect with the
            lab’s current work. You may also attach a current CV.
          </p>

          <p>
            Graduate applicants should apply through York University’s formal
            graduate admissions process. The undergraduate lab application
            form on this page is not used for MA or PhD admissions.
          </p>

          <p>
            <a
              href="https://www.yorku.ca/gradstudies/psychology/future-students/how-to-apply/"
              target="_blank"
              rel="noopener noreferrer">
              <span class="link-underline-text">Review the graduate application instructions</span> ↗
            </a>
          </p>
        </div>
      </details>

      <details class="faq-item">
        <summary>What qualities make a grad applicant more successful?</summary>

        <div class="faq-answer">
          <p>
            In principle, I'm interested in innovative research on any
            aspect of leadership, social status, group dynamics, and human
            connection, from theoretical questions about why people gain
            influence or become disconnected, to novel methods, including
            computational and natural-language approaches, for studying
            these questions in psychology, organizations, and everyday
            social life. Applied work that makes a genuine empirical
            contribution or sheds new light on an existing question is just
            as welcome as more theoretical work.
          </p>

          <p>
            Doing research in this space is challenging but rewarding. I
            look for originality of ideas, and I care most about working
            with students who are genuinely curious and motivated about
            research on human connection, rather than any particular prior
            background or credential.
          </p>
        </div>
      </details>

      <details class="faq-item">
        <summary>What kinds of research projects do grad students work on?</summary>

        <div class="faq-answer">
          <p>
            Student projects may examine leadership and status in groups,
            gender disparities, social connection and loneliness, cultural
            variation, or the ways social ideas are expressed in public
            discourse.
          </p>

          <p>
            Depending on the project, students may work with experiments,
            surveys, group-interaction data, multinational datasets, archival
            material, natural-language data, or computational methods.
          </p>

          <p>
            The format of lab activities varies by project. Some work may take
            place in person, while other meetings and research tasks may be
            completed remotely or in a hybrid format.
          </p>
        </div>
      </details>

      <details class="faq-item">
        <summary>
          What should I include when reaching out about applying as a prospective grad student?
        </summary>

        <div class="faq-answer">
          <p>
            Briefly describe your academic background (including any
            independent research projects or undergrad thesis research),
            research interests, and relevant coursework or experience.
            Importantly, describe one or two potential research questions you
            might be interested in pursuing.
          </p>

          <p>
            It is also helpful to include a CV or résumé, and an unofficial
            transcript. Also let us know whether you are interested in the MA
            or PhD program, or both.
          </p>
        </div>
      </details>

      <details class="faq-item">
        <summary>As a prospective grad or undergrad student, do I need prior experience with statistics or programming?</summary>

        <div class="faq-answer">
          <p>
            Prior experience is useful but is not required for every role.
            Different projects require different skills, and some positions
            are suitable for students who are still developing their research
            methods, statistics, or programming background.
          </p>

          <p>
            Applicants should accurately describe their current experience
            with tools such as R, Python, SPSS, Qualtrics, GitHub, data
            management, machine learning, natural-language processing, or
            qualitative coding. A strong interest in developing new technical
            and methodological skills is also important.
          </p>
        </div>
      </details>

      <details class="faq-item">
        <summary>What opportunities are available to undergrad students?</summary>

        <div class="faq-answer">
          <p>
            Undergraduate opportunities may be available through independent
            research projects, honours theses, volunteer research positions,
            or paid research-assistant roles.
          </p>

          <p>
            Opportunities depend on current projects, supervision capacity,
            relevant experience, and the amount of time a student can commit.
            Interested undergraduate students should complete the lab
            application rather than sending only a general expression of
            interest.
          </p>

          <p class="contact-link">
            <a
              class="application-button"
              href="https://docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQLScqWzKCojb5APpJ9pIF8_Rl01LVSMZ_BwrwAF5tNjEqCnhYcg/viewform?usp=header"
              target="_blank"
              rel="noopener noreferrer">
              Submit undergrad lab application
            </a>
          </p>
        </div>
      </details>

      <details class="faq-item">
        <summary>Can you write a reference letter for me?</summary>

        <div class="faq-answer">
          <p>
            I'm generally able to write letters for students I've worked with
            closely and who have been in the lab for at least two terms — for
            example, IRP students, thesis students, or others I've supervised
            directly. That kind of sustained experience lets me write a
            letter that speaks specifically and convincingly to your skills
            and potential.
          </p>

          <p>
            If our only interaction was through a course, I'm usually not in
            a strong position to write a detailed letter, since our more
            limited interaction means there is often little I can add beyond
            your grade. In that case, an instructor, TA, or supervisor who
            knows your work more closely may be able to write a stronger
            letter on your behalf.
          </p>

          <p>
            If you'd like to request a letter, please email me at least four
            weeks before your first deadline, with a reminder one week
            before that deadline. Please include your CV, your transcript,
            the programs or positions you're applying to, a list of the
            specific tasks and accomplishments from our work together, and a
            note of anything in particular you'd like me to be sure to
            include.
          </p>
        </div>
      </details>

    </div>
